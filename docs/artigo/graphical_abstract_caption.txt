A two-layer navigation architecture for substation inspection. A deliberative active-sensing patrol (ROS 2, Nav2, MPPI) carries the sensors across the site, while a reactive layer turns sensory saliency into an attraction/repulsion field that approaches anomalies and retreats from hazards, with a categorical rule giving the hazard channel precedence. Against a fixed patrol, which inspects none, it inspects all three anomalies. Attribution is explicit: the clearance gain on an on-route hazard is reproduced by the same keep-out without any reactive layer, so it is the keep-out that carries the safety result; what the reactive layer adds is the inspections, and what distinguishes it from a generic deviate-and-return heuristic is the precedence rule. Evaluation is in simulation with idealized perception; no contact-region entries were observed, though with 8-10 runs per configuration the 95% bound on that per-run probability remains 25.9-31.2%.
